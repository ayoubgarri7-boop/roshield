Scam guide

Spot the scam.

Seven very common scams on Roblox and Discord. For each one: how it works, how to spot it, and how to protect yourself. No method works 100% of the time, but these habits greatly reduce the risks.

01

Cookie logging

A hacker steals the “key” that keeps you logged in, and gets into your account without a password.

How it works

When you’re logged in, your browser keeps a small secret file (a cookie) that proves it’s really you. If a hacker copies it, they can take over your Roblox session without knowing your password. They steal it with a fake site, a file, or a malicious browser extension.

How to spot it

  • Someone says “look at my avatar,” “check out my game,” or “join my private server” and sends you a link.
  • The link takes you to a page that asks you to log in, download something, or install an extension.
  • The website address isn’t the real Roblox one (check it with this site’s link checker).

How to protect yourself

  • Never log in from a link someone sent you: type the official address yourself or use the app.
  • Don’t install extensions or files from someone you don’t know.
  • If you have any doubt, sign out of your other sessions (look in your security settings) and change your password from the official app.
02

Fake “free Robux” offers and Robux generators

You’re promised free Robux, but the goal is to steal your account or your money.

How it works

A website or video claims it can give you free Robux. To “receive” them, it asks for your username, your password, a code, a download, or several surveys. In reality, nothing arrives: the scammers just collect your information.

How to spot it

  • Huge promises, like “10,000 free Robux” or “100% working generator.”
  • A countdown, or a message like “only 3 spots left,” to rush you.
  • The site asks for your password, wants you to “prove you’re human,” or tells you to download a file.

How to protect yourself

  • Remember one simple rule: there’s no such thing as a Robux generator. The only ways to get Robux are the official ones.
  • Close the page, and never enter your password on a site that promises gifts.
  • If it’s a video or a message, report it and warn your friends.
03

Fake moderators and fake support

Someone pretends to be Roblox or Discord staff to scare you and make you act fast.

How it works

You get a message saying “your account is going to be banned” or “we detected a problem.” The person then asks you to share your screen, give them a code, or click a link to “fix it.” When you share your screen, you show them your codes and your account.

How to spot it

  • A direct message with an urgent or threatening tone, like “you have 10 minutes.”
  • You’re asked for your password, a code sent by text message or email, or to share your screen.
  • The message comes from a regular user, or from an account with a name that copies a real team’s.

How to protect yourself

  • Never share your screen with someone who contacts you first, especially while you’re logging in.
  • Never give out a password or a code. Real staff will never ask you for them.
  • If you’re not sure, contact support through the official website, typing the address yourself.
04

Fake job offers and “test my game” requests

You’re offered a job or a game test, but the file you’re told to run hides malware.

How it works

Someone offers you a job as a tester, developer, or moderator, or asks you to test their game. To do that, they send you a file to run on your computer (a program, a .exe, a .zip). That file is malware that can steal your passwords and your sessions.

How to spot it

  • The offer arrives in a direct message you didn’t ask for, with a very generous reward.
  • You’re told to download a file from outside Roblox, sometimes after turning off your antivirus.
  • The person pressures you, or refuses to test inside the official Roblox game.

How to protect yourself

  • Never run a file sent by someone you don’t know, even if it looks like a game.
  • Never turn off your antivirus because someone tells you to.
  • A real game test happens inside Roblox, with a link to the official site. Show the offer to a trusted adult.
05

Fake trades and fake middlemen

You’re talked into handing over your items first, through a “trusted middleman” who is really an accomplice.

How it works

For an item trade, someone says it’s safer to use a middleman they know. In reality, that person is an accomplice: you hand over your items and get nothing back.

How to spot it

  • You’re offered a very generous trade, but asked to give first.
  • You’re pushed to use “a trusted middleman” chosen by the other person.
  • You’re shown fake proof (screenshots) to reassure you.

How to protect yourself

  • Only trade through the official trading system, never outside it.
  • Refuse any middleman suggested by the other person, even if lots of people vouch for them.
  • If an offer looks too good to be true, say no: you lose nothing by refusing.
06

Fake “free Nitro” gifts on Discord

A message promises a free subscription, but the link steals your account.

How it works

You get a direct message, sometimes from a friend whose account was hacked, offering you Nitro or a gift. The link leads to a fake page that asks you to log in or scan a QR code. By scanning it, you give access to your account.

How to spot it

  • A message like “here, free Nitro for you” or “you received a gift.”
  • The link doesn’t go to an official Discord site, or you’re asked to scan a QR code.
  • A friend who talks differently than usual, with one short sentence and a link.

How to protect yourself

  • Never scan a QR code that someone else gives you: it’s used to log your account in somewhere else.
  • Never log in from a link someone sent you. Always go through the official app.
  • If a friend sends you this, warn them another way: their account may have been hacked.
07

People who tell you to paste code into the browser console

You’re given a piece of code to copy into your browser, and it hands your account to a stranger.

How it works

Someone tells you “open the console (F12), paste this code, and you’ll get Robux, a rare item, or an unban.” The console lets you run commands inside your session: if you paste code you don’t understand, you let a stranger act in your place.

How to spot it

  • Instructions like “press F12, go to Console, paste this, and press Enter.”
  • A promise of free stuff, a rare item, or an unban in return.
  • The code is long and unreadable, and the person insists that you do it fast.

How to protect yourself

  • Never paste code into the browser console because someone asks you to.
  • Close the page and don’t go back, even if the person keeps insisting.
  • If you already did it, sign out of your other sessions (look in your security settings) and change your password right away.

Think you fell for a scam?

Don’t type anything, change your password from the official app, and sign out of your other sessions (look in your security settings). Talk to a trusted adult: there’s no shame in it, and it happens to adults too. You can also check a link or read the security checklist.
